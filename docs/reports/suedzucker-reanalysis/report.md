# Südzucker: where the loss came from, and what the project can verify

**Information cutoff: 8 November 2026.** Sugar accounted for EUR 164 million of the group's EUR 187 million year-on-year decline in operating result in FY 2025/26. At group level, the first half of FY 2026/27 shows an earnings recovery; Sugar itself still lost money. There is direct evidence of regional processing interruptions and a weaker subsequent crop outlook, but no evidence here of group-wide supply failure or a specific customer default.

## 1. Is the weakness concentrated?

EUR million. FY 2025/26 ran from 1 March 2025 to 28 February 2026. The financial reader reconciled each year's five segment amounts to the group for revenue, operating EBITDA and operating result. Figures below use operating result, not EBITDA. [Annual report, physical pp. 6, 33–35](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Suedzucker_Geschaeftsbericht_2025-26.pdf#page=33)

| Segment | FY revenue 2024/25 → 2025/26 | Operating result 2024/25 → 2025/26 | Change in operating result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sugar | 3,876 → 2,786 | −13 → −177 | −164 |
| Special products | 2,275 → 2,216 | 203 → 177 | −26 |
| CropEnergies | 959 → 793 | 22 → 37 | +15 |
| Starch | 955 → 911 | 36 → 21 | −15 |
| Fruit | 1,629 → 1,646 | 102 → 105 | +3 |
| **Group** | **9,694 → 8,352** | **350 → 163** | **−187** |

Sugar contributed 81.2% of the net revenue decline and 87.7% of the net operating-result decline, calculated from the segment table. These are accounting contributions, not causal estimates. The issuer reports lower sugar selling prices and volumes; falling production costs did not offset them. Special products alone earned EUR 177 million, offsetting the Sugar operating loss of the same size. Fruit also remained profitable. [Sugar business review, physical pp. 44–45](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Suedzucker_Geschaeftsbericht_2025-26.pdf#page=44)

**Profit versus cash.** Group operating EBITDA fell from 723 to 535; ROCE fell from 5.2% to 2.7%. The operating result of +163 becomes a result from operating activities of −361 after restructuring/special items (−517) and the equity-accounted result (−7). Group loss after tax was 378, versus 86 previously. The Sugar impairment of EUR 427 million is already reflected in special items; adding it again would overstate the loss. Operating cash flow fell from 906 to 462, compared with 450 invested in property, plant, equipment and intangibles. The working-capital release of 161 was helped by an expanded factoring programme. Net financial debt rose from 1,654 to 1,750. Positive cash generation alone is weak evidence of an operating recovery. [Annual report, physical pp. 33–39](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Suedzucker_Geschaeftsbericht_2025-26.pdf#page=35)

## 2. What actually happened along the chain?

- **2025 crop:** cultivation area fell from 373,800 to 305,800 ha (−18.2%), partly a deliberate adjustment to demand. Beet processed fell from 29.0 to 24.8 million tonnes (−14.5%). Theoretical sugar yield rose from 12.1 to 13.9 t/ha (+15.1%). Total sugar production including refining was 3.8 versus 3.9 million tonnes (reported change −2.8% on unrounded values). Consolidated sugar sales were 3.7 versus 4.4 million tonnes. Declines in processed beet, sugar output and sales are different observations. [Chart and operating commentary, physical p. 45 / printed p. 43](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Suedzucker_Geschaeftsbericht_2025-26.pdf#page=45)
- **Processing:** the issuer says that processing was mostly uninterrupted. Frost affected quality in parts of Germany and Poland; adverse weather delayed harvesting and interrupted processing in Romania and Moldova. There is no named supplier failure or missed customer delivery established by those facts. An independently sourced Wabern processing permit identifies the operator and input, not actual deliveries or plant utilisation. [Annual report, physical p. 45](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-05/Suedzucker_Geschaeftsbericht_2025-26.pdf#page=45) · [Wabern permit](https://rp-kassel.hessen.de/sites/rp-kassel.hessen.de/files/2024-12/abschrift_bescheid_suedzucker_ag_wabern_siloerweiterung.pdf)
- **Subsequent crop:** the July report forecast lower FY sugar production and sales volumes because of reduced acreage. In October the issuer described drought and pest/disease pressure in southern Germany and France and lowered yield expectations. This is prospective crop and factory-utilisation exposure; the expected yield is not a realised supplier shortfall. [Q1, p. 17](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-07/Q1_2026-27_EN.pdf#page=17) · [H1, p. 12](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Suedzucker_Q2_2026-27_en.pdf#page=12)
- **Downstream:** the H1 report shows better volume for major starch products alongside lower starch and sweetener prices. Higher ethanol margins helped earnings. This is evidence about mix, price and demand, not the terms of individual customer contracts. [H1, p. 16](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/Suedzucker_Q2_2026-27_en.pdf#page=16)

## 3. Did the later evidence change the diagnosis?

The October H1 release covers March–August 2026. Group revenue was almost flat (4,199 → 4,188), while operating EBITDA rose from 189 to 303. In the case replay, Sugar revenue fell 1,389 → 1,269 and its operating result improved −89 → −63; Starch revenue fell 474 → 456, but its operating result rose 5 → 12. These are actual half-year changes. Neither the EUR 26 million improvement in Sugar nor the EUR 7 million improvement in Starch can be assigned to a single alert. **Attributable revenue, attributable profit and contract fulfilment remain unknown.** Group guidance at the October release was revenue of EUR 8.3–8.7 billion and operating EBITDA of EUR 540–680 million for FY 2026/27, not realised results. [Issuer H1 release and outlook](https://www.suedzuckergroup.com/en/press/sudzucker-significant-increase-operating-ebitda-first-half-fiscal-202627)

## 4. International policy: distinguish status from effect

**Trade.** The European Commission confirms provisional application of the EU–Mercosur interim agreement from 1 May 2026; the implementing regulation covers sugar and ethanol tariff quotas. Sugar and CropEnergies therefore have identifiable exposure to changes in import competition. A quota is not an import volume, an import volume is not the company's lost volume, and a market price is not its realised price. Company-specific effects remain unknown. This policy check was performed separately from the tracker. [Commission: provisional application](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/provisional-application-eu-mercosur-interim-trade-agreement-begins-1-may-2026-2026-04-24_en) · [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/996](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/996/oj/eng)

**Beverage taxation.** The archived October 8 cutoff records a German coalition proposal. By November 8, the government had backed a proposal and it was under parliamentary consideration. Pure juices and pure juice concentrates would be exempt under the cabinet proposal. France already has its own schedule; EU policy coordination does not establish a common EU rate. A German drinks maker might reformulate and buy less liquid sugar, but the company-specific taxable litres, recipes and sourcing shares are unknown. The Fruit segment's sales to the beverage industry cannot be treated as wholly taxable. This was a previously assigned research question, not an automatically confirmed event affecting a named customer. [German cabinet proposal](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/zuckergetraenkesteuer-2457018) · [French tax schedule](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/11575-PGP.html/identifiant%3DBOI-BAREME-000038-20251224) · [Commission policy, p. 13](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/dfb60cde-21a5-426d-8616-e394a326abc2_en?filename=ncd_com-2025-1024_act_en.pdf#page=13)

Capital allocation remained an uncovered narrative topic. The annual-only result is marked `needs_review`. The diagnosis above combines those outputs with explicitly checked original tables and operating commentary. [Annual output](annual.json)
2. **Generic product-to-segment audit.** A separate all-page pass used native text on 233 pages, layout on 44 and OCR on one. It returned **28 distinct unknown candidates and zero supported product-to-segment links**. The filing defines the five businesses on physical p. 202 under *Segmentbericht*. The note-title matcher recognises *Segmentberichterstattung* but not this title; its definition set was empty. The reconciled financial table still has five segments, through a different reader. Success on earlier issuers does not establish German-language graph coverage. [Full product audit](product-audit.json)
3. **Previously reviewed case replay.** Rebuilt both dated snapshots from fingerprinted annual/interim disclosures and public documents. The local outputs match the archived P1 evidence, paths and values when its prior Neo4j readback field is excluded. No new database projection was performed. The two October alerts (beet and starch sweeteners) are retained in November, not reissued as new. The case uses a reviewed mapping; its graph is not the output of the generic annual-only audit. 4. **Fresh tracking from the PDF alone.** In an empty work directory with no supplied links, the planner selected beet acreage/yield, pricing of new starch contracts and the EU–Mercosur agreement. The new run found and admitted the July and October issuer reports. It generated **zero new alerts on 21 May, 8 October and 8 November**; the three fulfilment states remained unknown. The July and October originals were excluded from the May evidence set. It did **not** independently reproduce the archived October alerts. The planner treated a reported 2025 acreage as a plan; a proposed starch claim cited a row without its metric and period headings and did not answer the question about contract repricing. The policy task sought both a court ruling and confirmation of provisional application; a delivery-evidence check was inappropriate for this legal-status question. These are observable planning and review failures, not evidence that the exposures disappeared. ## Next evidence to seek

Watch actual harvested beet tonnes and quality by region, campaign length and idle days, then compare them with the company's guidance. Track realised sugar and starch prices separately from volume and ethanol margins. Check utilisation of the relevant EU tariff quotas alongside EU white-sugar prices; require direct issuer or customer evidence before changing fulfilment or attributing a euro amount. For capital allocation, compare site cash flows and avoidable costs before ranking closure or investment options.
